## Context

Customers message businesses everywhere now: a DM on Instagram, a WhatsApp message at midnight, an email the next morning. For a small team that means many open tabs, many logins, and a real chance that a question slips through.

## Problem

The big support suites were not designed for small and growing teams. They are expensive, and they make a small team glue several tools together just to keep up with its own customers.

## My role

I designed and built Chatonics as a product: the positioning, the feature set, the product experience and the public website at [chatonics.com](https://chatonics.com).

## Approach

- **One inbox first.** Every channel lands in one thread-per-customer view, so the team works one queue instead of many apps.
- **AI that stays grounded.** The AI agent drafts replies from the team's own knowledge base, can answer after hours, and can suggest a reply for a person to approve.
- **Everything a support team needs in one product.** Tickets, knowledge, analytics and team chat sit next to the inbox instead of in separate tools.

## What I built

- **AI agent and workspace copilot** that drafts grounded replies and answers questions about the workspace.
- **Unified shared inbox** for WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, Telegram, email, SMS, Discord, Viber and TikTok.
- **Live chat widget** for the team's own website, with live visitor presence.
- **Ticketing and SLA timers** to track and prioritize conversations.
- **Knowledge base** that works as a help center and grounds the AI.
- **Analytics** for response time, resolution rate and channel mix.
- **Two-way translation** so agents and customers each write in their own language.
- **Internal team chat** with presence status.

## Outcome

Chatonics is live at [chatonics.com](https://chatonics.com). Teams can start on a free plan without a credit card and connect their channels to one inbox.

## What I learned

Building a product end to end is the fastest way to test product judgment. Every decision about scope, pricing and AI behavior shows up directly in what a small team can actually use.
